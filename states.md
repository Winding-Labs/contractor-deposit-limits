# Contractor deposit laws, state by state

Every state's rule on how much a contractor may ask for up front on a home improvement job, with the law and what it says. The short version is in the [README](README.md); the same data is in [CSV](data/deposit-limits.csv) and [JSON](data/deposit-limits.json). Statutes checked November 11, 2026. Not legal advice.

## Alabama

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap

**Applies to:** Licensed residential home builders/remodelers (the license rule applies when a job costs more than $10,000)

**Law:** [Ala. Code Title 34, Ch. 14A (Home Builders Licensure Law)](https://hblb.alabama.gov/law/)

We read the full licensure law on the Home Builders Licensure Board's site: nothing on deposits, down payments or advance payments. It requires licensees to use a valid written contract (§34-14A-8(f)). There is a Homeowners' Recovery Fund. No agency source discusses deposit size.

## Alaska

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap

**Applies to:** Registered contractors; a residential endorsement is needed for 1–4 unit homes

**Law:** [AS 08.18 (Construction Contractors)](https://law.alaska.gov/department/civil/consumer/home_improvement.html)

AS 08.18 (read in full via akleg.gov print view) has no deposit rule; 'deposit' there only means a cash deposit in place of a bond. The Dept. of Law consumer guide gives advice only: 'Never pay for the entire job in advance' and pay on agreed terms. It also mentions a 5-day cancellation right for door-to-door sales.

## Arizona

**Deposit limit:** No general cap; 15% for residential in-ground pools and spas

**Applies to:** §32-1158: contracts over $1,000 between a contractor and an owner. §32-1158.01: licensed contractor building a residential in-ground pool or spa for a purchaser who lives (or will live) in the residence

**Law:** [A.R.S. §32-1158 (contract contents); §32-1158.01(A)(2) (pools/spas)](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/01158-01.htm)

§32-1158(A)(7)-(8): the contract must state the dollar amount of any advance deposit and of each progress payment, with the construction stage that triggers it (a disclosure, not a cap). Pool payment schedule: ≤15% at signing; ≤25% after excavation; ≤25% after steel/plumbing/concrete; ≤25% after decking; balance before interior finish. The schedule can be changed if the buyer requires a payment and performance bond (§32-1158.01(B)). Breaking the pool rule violates §32-1154(A).

## Arkansas

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap

**Applies to:** Residential building contractors and home improvement/remodelers licensed or registered by the Contractors Licensing Board

**Law:** [Ark. Code Ann. §17-25-501 et seq. (Residential Building Contractors)](https://labor.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/Residential-Contractor-Law-revised-04.2026.pdf)

This is the Board's published copy of the residential contractor law, revised 04/2026, not the official code. A full-text search found no deposit, down-payment or advance-payment provision. The official Arkansas Code is on Lexis and was not opened.

## California

**Deposit limit:** Lesser of 10% of the contract price or $1,000

**Applies to:** Home improvement contracts by licensed contractors, including swimming pools

**Law:** [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §7159.5(a)(3); notice required by §7159(d)(8)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-7159-5/)

Read on FindLaw ('current as of Jan 1, 2026'); leginfo and Justia were blocked. §7159.5(a)(5): apart from the down payment, the contractor may not ask for or accept payment beyond the value of work performed. (a)(4): payment schedule in dollars and cents. (a)(8): contractors with a blanket performance and payment bond (or approved joint control) are exempt from (a)(3)-(5). The contract must say in bold: 'THE DOWNPAYMENT MAY NOT EXCEED $1,000 OR 10 PERCENT OF THE CONTRACT PRICE, WHICHEVER IS LESS' (§7159(d)(8)(C)). Pools: the $200 / 2% lead is not in current §7159, §7159.5, §7167 or §7151.2. §7167 voids pool contracts that don't substantially comply with §7159(d)(8), so the same $1,000 / 10% rule applies. Also confirmed in CSLB, "Contracting for Success" (https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/GuidesAndPublications/ContractingForSuccess.pdf): "The down payment may not exceed $1,000 or 10 percent of the contract amount, whichever is less."

## Colorado

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap found

**Applies to:** All building, construction or remodeling contracts (trust rule); residential roofing contracts (roofing rules)

**Law:** [C.R.S. §38-22-127 (trust funds); C.R.S. §6-22-101 et seq. (roofing)](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/olls/crs2024-title-38.pdf)

We didn't find a statewide cap, but we couldn't confirm there isn't one. Check with your state attorney general's consumer office. Colorado has no general home-improvement statute, and we found no agency statement on deposit size. Related rules, verified from the 2024 C.R.S. (uncertified printout): §38-22-127 says money paid to a contractor is held in trust for subcontractors and suppliers, and misusing it is theft. Roofing: §6-22-103(1)(f) requires a 72-hour rescission clause with a full deposit refund. §6-22-103(2) requires a bold statement that the roofer holds the owner's payment in trust until materials are delivered or most of the work is done. §6-22-104: the owner can rescind within 72 hours of an insurance denial, and payments are refunded within 10 days. Amendments after 2024 were not checked.

*Not yet confirmed against the statute. Before you pay a large deposit, ask the Colorado attorney general's consumer protection office.*

## Connecticut

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap

**Applies to:** Home improvement contracts with registered contractors

**Law:** [Conn. Gen. Stat. §20-418 et seq. (Home Improvement Act), esp. §20-429 and §20-427](https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_400.htm)

We read the whole chapter: no deposit cap. §20-429(a) requires a written, signed contract with start and completion dates and cancellation rights, or it is unenforceable. §20-427(b)(8): the contractor must refund within 10 days of a written request if no substantial work has been done and more than 30 days have passed since the contract's start date (or the contract date if none). New homes (ch. 399a, §20-417d(d)(7)): a similar 10-day deposit refund rule; no cap. Both chapters have guaranty funds.

## Delaware

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap

**Applies to:** New residential construction and residential improvements

**Law:** [6 Del. C. ch. 36 (Home Construction and Improvement Protection); 6 Del. C. ch. 35 (Building Construction Payments)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c036/index.html)

Ch. 36 has no deposit cap; §3602 requires escrow of the cost of unfinished work at a new-home settlement. Ch. 35 (read in the Title 6 PDF): §3502 makes all money a contractor receives, including deposits, trust funds; §3503 says it must first go to paying labor and material suppliers; §3505 sets criminal penalties. We did not find a separate Delaware home improvement contract statute.

## District of Columbia

**Deposit limit:** No percentage cap, but only a licensed home improvement contractor may take any payment before the work is finished

**Applies to:** Home improvement contracts in DC (16 DCMR 899 defines these as $300 or more)

**Law:** [16 DCMR §800.1; §808.1, 808.8, 808.10](https://dcregs.dc.gov/Common/DCMR/RuleDetail.aspx?RuleId=R0028912)

Official DCRegs text ('View text' files for 16-800 and 16-808; source 56 DCR 3098, 2009). 800.1: no one may require or accept any payment before full completion unless licensed. 808.1: no payment before the agreement is put in writing. 808.8: if the contractor rejects the contract, payments are refunded. 808.10: the contract must state payment terms and start/finish dates. DC courts have held that an unlicensed contractor who takes advance payments has a void contract. The 'one-third' lead was not found.

## Florida

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap; an initial payment over 10% starts permit (30 days) and start-of-work (90 days) deadlines

**Applies to:** Any contractor (licensed or not) repairing, improving or building residential real property

**Law:** [Fla. Stat. §489.126](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0489/Sections/0489.126.html)

2026 Fla. Stat. §489.126(2)(a): if the initial payment is more than 10% of the contract price, the contractor must apply for permits within 30 days and start work within 90 days after permits issue, unless there is just cause or the payer agreed in writing to a longer period. (2)(b): the owner can demand by certified mail that the contractor apply, start or refund; no response in 30 days lets a court infer there was no just cause. (3): a contractor holding money beyond the value of work done may not stop work for any 90-day period. (5)-(6): penalties from first-degree misdemeanor up to first-degree felony depending on the amount. New homes: §501.1375 requires builders/developers (10+ units a year) to escrow buyers' deposits, up to 10% of the price, on one- or two-family homes; the buyer can waive this in writing.

## Hawaii

## Illinois

## Maine

**Applies to:** Home construction contracts over $3,000 (build, remodel or repair a home of 3 or fewer units, including electrical, plumbing, heating, carpeting and windows); not commercial buildings

## New York

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap on the amount, but payments before substantial completion must be held in escrow or bonded (contracts over $500)

**Applies to:** Home improvement contracts over $500 (GBL §770(6)) for residential property; includes custom homes but not other new-home sales/construction (§770(3))

**Law:** [N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §771(1)(e)-(g); Lien Law §71-a(4); GBL §770(6)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LIE/71-A)

No cap on the amount. Payments received before substantial completion must go into an escrow account at an in-state depositary within 5 business days and remain the owner's property (Lien Law §71-a(4)(a)). The owner must be told the depositary within 10 business days if the contract doesn't name it. Instead of escrow, the contractor may post a bond, contract of indemnity or irrevocable letter of credit, delivered within 10 business days (§71-a(4)(b)). Withdrawals may not exceed the contract's payment schedule (§71-a(4)(e)). The contract must give notice of the deposit duty (GBL §771(1)(e)). Progress payments must 'bear a reasonable relationship' to the work (§771(1)(f)). Time-and-materials payments are exempt from the escrow duty (§771(1)(g)). Note: GBL §771-a is about financing disclosure, not escrow.

*Not yet confirmed against the statute. Before you pay a large deposit, ask the North Carolina attorney general's consumer protection office.*

## North Dakota

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap

**Applies to:** Licensed contractors (license required for jobs over $4,000)

**Law:** [N.D.C.C. ch. 43-07 (Contractors), esp. §§43-07-02(3), 43-07-14(1)(a),(f)](https://www.ndlegis.gov/cencode/t43c07.pdf)

We read the full chapter text: no deposit cap. Related rules: abandonment after a deposit is grounds for a license complaint (43-07-14(1)(a)). A rebuttable presumption of abandonment arises if work isn't substantially started within 60 days of the written start date (90 days from contract date if none), or not completed within 90 days of the written completion date (180 days if none). Failing to fully refund the advance payment once that presumption arises and a refund is requested is a separate ground (43-07-14(1)(f)). Willfully abandoning a project after receiving payment is 'construction fraud' (43-07-02(3)(c)); refunding before charges are filed is a defense (43-07-02(4)(a)).

## Ohio

**Deposit limit:** 10% of the contract price, plus up to 75% of non-returnable special-order items (contracts over $25,000)

**Applies to:** 'Home construction service contracts' over $25,000 for new construction or repair/improvement/remodel/renovation of a 1-3 family dwelling (not 4+ unit buildings except individual units, not condo common areas, not manufactured/mobile/industrialized units). 'Supplier' is defined as one who maintains at least $250,000 general liability insurance.

**Law:** [Ohio Rev. Code 4722.04 (definitions in 4722.01)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4722.04)

The rule does not apply to cost-plus contracts. The supplier may accept construction-loan draws. Contracts of $25,000 or more must be written and list the anticipated start and completion dates (4722.02). Owner remedies are in 4722.08. The Home Solicitation Sales Act (R.C. 1345.21 et seq.) is a cancellation rule, not a cap. Not opened: whether an uninsured contractor escapes the cap because of the 'supplier' definition; no case law checked. R.C. 4722.01(C) defines a home construction service contract as one "for an amount exceeding twenty-five thousand dollars."

## Oklahoma

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap found

**Law:** [15 O.S. §§765.1–765.3 (Home Repair Fraud Act); 59 O.S. §1151.21 (Roofing Contractor Registration Act)](https://www.oklegislature.gov/OK_Statutes/CompleteTitles/os15.rtf)

We didn't find a statewide cap, but we couldn't confirm there isn't one. Check with your state attorney general's consumer office. The Home Repair Fraud Act (residential repair/improvement, not original construction) prohibits misrepresentation and charging a price that 'unreasonably exceeds' the value of the work (765.3(A)(1)(c)), but sets no deposit cap. Roofing paid by insurance (59 O.S. §1151.21): the owner may cancel within 72 hours after written notice of a claim denial, and payments must be returned within 10 days, less emergency services. ## Oregon

**Deposit limit:** No hard cap; a deposit over 50% can lead to license probation if the contractor then fails to perform or refund

**Applies to:** CCB-licensed contractors offering to perform a home improvement

**Law:** [ORS 701.312 (ORS ch. 701, 2025 ed.)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors701.html)

ORS 701.312 lets the Construction Contractors Board place a contractor on probation if it 'accepts a deposit of more than 50 percent of the total contract price' and either fails to perform diligently per the contract or fails to perform and doesn't return the deposit within 10 days of a reasonable demand. This is a disciplinary trigger, not a ban. We found no other deposit limit in ch. 701. ORS 701.620–701.640 set prompt-payment rules (progress and final payments).

## Pennsylvania

**Deposit limit:** One-third of the contract price, plus special-order materials named in the contract (contracts over $5,000)

**Applies to:** Home improvement contracts over $5,000 with owners of residential property (1-2 unit dwellings, single units incl. condos/co-ops); excludes new-home construction and contractors with under $5,000 in home improvements the previous year

**Law:** [73 P.S. §517.9(10) (HICPA §9(10)); also §517.7(a)(9), §517.9(9)](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Act_132_Home_Improvement.pdf)

Your lead had it wrong: the cap is in §517.9(10), not §517.7, and the threshold is $5,000, not $1,000 (confirmed in the AG's compiled text, amended through 10/22/14, and on FindLaw, current through 1/1/2026). The cap does not apply to home improvement retailers that post an irrevocable letter of credit ($100,000 per store, up to $2M) and use registered, insured contractors (§517.9(10)(ii)). No payment may be demanded or received before the contract is signed (§517.9(9)). The contract must list the down payment and the special-order materials cost separately (§517.7(a)(9)). 'Special order material' (§517.2) means non-stock, factory-ordered, non-returnable items. Misrepresenting an item as special-order is home improvement fraud (§517.8(a)(6)).

## Rhode Island

Contracts must contain the mechanics' lien notice and right-of-rescission notice (§5-65-3).

## Tennessee

**Deposit limit:** One-third of the contract price (home improvement over $3,000, in nine counties)

**Applies to:** 'Home improvement' contracts over $3,000 (repair/remodel/addition to residential land or buildings; excludes new home construction) by any home improvement contractor, licensed or not (§62-6-501(4),(6)), where the Home Improvement part applies

**Law:** [Tenn. Code Ann. §62-6-510(12) (also (11))](https://codes.findlaw.com/tn/title-62-professions-businesses-and-trades/tn-code-sect-62-6-510/)

Exceptions: (A) the contract lets the contractor furnish a performance and payment bond (or lien-and-completion bond or equivalent) covering full performance, of at least 1% of the contractor's annual net home-improvement labor sales; or (B) after being fully advised in writing of the right to withhold up to 100% of payment before completion, the owner elects to prepay or 'the parties agree on a schedule of payments.' Exception (B) makes the cap waivable in practice. No payment may be demanded or received before the contract is signed (§62-6-510(11)). Scope caveat: §62-6-516(b) lets counties opt in, and TDCI pages (blocked our automated check) reportedly say the law applies only in adopting counties: Bradley, Davidson, Hamilton, Haywood, Knox, Marion, Robertson, Rutherford, Shelby. The FindLaw text is 'current as of January 02, 2024'. County scope confirmed on the Board for Licensing Contractors home improvement page (https://www.tn.gov/commerce/regboards/contractors/license/get/home-improvement.html).

## Texas

It does not apply if the contractor has had a physical business address in the county or an adjacent county for at least 1 year (§58.002). §162.006: written contracts over $5,000 to improve a residential homestead.

**Law:** [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §§58.001–58.003; Tex. Prop. Code §162.006](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/business-and-commerce-code/bus-com-sect-58-003/)

We didn't find a statewide cap, but we couldn't confirm there isn't one. Check with your state attorney general's consumer office. For ordinary remodeling we found no general cap, but no official source says there is none; TexasLawHelp only advises 'Don't pay the full amount for the project up front.' §58.003(b)(1): a disaster remediation contractor 'may not require a person to make a full or partial payment under a contract before the contractor begins work.' Prop. Code §162.006(a): homestead improvement contracts over $5,000 require the contractor to deposit the owner's payments (trust funds) in a 'construction account' at a financial institution (FindLaw current as of 1/1/2026).

## Vermont

**Deposit limit:** Half of the labor cost or half of the materials price, whichever is greater (maximum-price contracts)

**Applies to:** Contracts by contractors registered with the Office of Professional Regulation for residential construction on dwellings of up to 4 units (registration required for residential construction over $10,000 incl. labor and materials, §5501)

**Law:** [26 V.S.A. §5509(c) (Residential Contractors, ch. 106)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/26/106/05509)

Exact text: 'the contract may require a down payment of up to one-half of the cost of labor to the consumer, or one-half of the price of materials, whichever is greater' (§5509(c)(1)). Uncapped time-and-materials contracts: 'a down payment as negotiated by the parties' (§5509(c)(2)). A written contract (price, estimated start and completion dates, scope, change-order terms) is required 'prior to receiving a deposit or commencing' work when estimated labor plus materials exceed $10,000 (§5509(b)). Effective July 1, 2022 (Act 182, 2021 Adj. Sess.). It is unclear whether (c) also binds a registrant's contracts of $10,000 or less.

## Virginia

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap

**Applies to:** Residential contracting by licensed contractors (single- or multi-family residential buildings and accessory structures)

**Law:** [18 VAC 50-22-260(B)(8) (Board for Contractors standards of practice); Va. Code §18.2-200.1](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title18/agency50/chapter22/section260/)

No cap. ## Wisconsin

**Deposit limit:** No statewide cap

**Applies to:** Home improvement of residential or noncommercial property; excludes new-residence construction and 'major renovation' (contract price above the structure's assessed value)

**Law:** [Wis. Admin. Code ch. ATCP 110 (Home Improvement Practices), esp. 110.05(1)(a), (3); 110.07; 110.025; 110.027](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/atcp/090/110/07)

No cap. Any contract requiring payment before completion must be written (110.05(1)(a)), and the buyer must get a copy before the seller starts work or takes payment (110.05(3)). If the buyer prepays and the seller misses the contract deadline, fails to give delay notice, or (with no deadline) is untimely, the buyer may cancel, demand return of unexpended payments within 15 calendar days, demand delivery of materials bought with the buyer's money, and get a written accounting (110.07). The seller must give timely written notice of delays, and new deadlines need the buyer's written agreement (110.027). Lien waivers are due with partial payments on request (110.025). Buyers can sue for twice the loss under Wis. Stat. §100.20(5).
