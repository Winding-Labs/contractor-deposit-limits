# Contractor deposit limits by state (2026)

**How much can a contractor ask for up front?** In most of the US there is no legal cap on a home improvement deposit. Ten states cap it on some contracts: **California and Nevada** at 10% of the price or $1,000, whichever is less; **Maryland, Maine, Massachusetts, Pennsylvania and Tennessee** at one-third; **Ohio** at 10% on large contracts; **Vermont** at half; and **Arizona** at 15% for pools and spas. Everywhere else, a deposit above one-third of the price is the red flag state consumer guides warn about.

This repository is an open dataset of every state's rule (all 50 states and DC), with the statute, who it covers and what else the law says about advance payments. Statutes checked **November 11, 2026**. [Every state in detail](states.md) · [CSV](data/deposit-limits.csv) · [JSON](data/deposit-limits.json)

## The 10 states with a deposit cap

| State | Deposit limit | Law |
|---|---|---|
| [Arizona](states.md#arizona) | No general cap; 15% for residential in-ground pools and spas | [A.R.S. §32-1158 (contract contents); §32-1158.01(A)(2) (pools/spas)](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/01158-01.htm) |
| [California](states.md#california) | Lesser of 10% of the contract price or $1,000 | [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §7159.5(a)(3); notice required by §7159(d)(8)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-7159-5/) |
| [Maine](states.md#maine) | One-third of the contract price (contracts over $3,000) | [10 M.R.S. §1487(5); definitions §1486](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/10/title10sec1487.html) |
| [Maryland](states.md#maryland) | One-third of the contract price | [Md. Code, Bus. Reg. §8-617(b)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gbr&section=8-617&enactments=false) |
| [Massachusetts](states.md#massachusetts) | One-third of the contract price, or the cost of special-order materials if that is more (contracts over $1,000) | [M.G.L. c.142A §2(a)(6)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter142A/Section2) |
| [Nevada](states.md#nevada) | Lesser of 10% of the contract price or $1,000 (owner-occupied single-family homes, pools/spas and residential solar) | [NRS 624.970(2)(g) (residential improvements); NRS 624.940(2)(g) (residential pools/spas); NRS 624.875(2)(g) (residential photovoltaic)](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-624.html) |
| [Ohio](states.md#ohio) | 10% of the contract price, plus up to 75% of non-returnable special-order items (contracts over $25,000) | [Ohio Rev. Code 4722.04 (definitions in 4722.01)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4722.04) |
| [Pennsylvania](states.md#pennsylvania) | One-third of the contract price, plus special-order materials named in the contract (contracts over $5,000) | [73 P.S. §517.9(10) (HICPA §9(10)); also §517.7(a)(9), §517.9(9)](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Act_132_Home_Improvement.pdf) |
| [Tennessee](states.md#tennessee) | One-third of the contract price (home improvement over $3,000, in nine counties) | [Tenn. Code Ann. §62-6-510(12) (also (11))](https://codes.findlaw.com/tn/title-62-professions-businesses-and-trades/tn-code-sect-62-6-510/) |
| [Vermont](states.md#vermont) | Half of the labor cost or half of the materials price, whichever is greater (maximum-price contracts) | [26 V.S.A. §5509(c) (Residential Contractors, ch. 106)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/26/106/05509) |

The cap usually applies only to home improvement contracts by licensed contractors, and several have a minimum contract size or exceptions for special-order materials and bonded contractors. Read the state's entry before you rely on it.

## How to use it

1. **Find your state** above and open its entry for who the rule covers. Many caps apply only to home improvement on an owner-occupied home, or only above a contract size.
2. **Do the math.** On a $30,000 kitchen in California, the most a contractor may take at signing is $1,000. In Maryland it is $10,000.
3. **Tie the rest to the work.** Some states also police the payments after the deposit: California bars payments beyond the value of the work done, New York makes the contractor escrow or bond money paid before the job is substantially complete, and Florida sets permit and start-of-work deadlines once the first payment is over 10%. Pay in stages that match finished work, never in cash, and never the full price up front.
4. **If the deposit is over the cap,** say so in writing and cite the statute. In some states the contract must state the limit itself (California's must say it in bold).

## Data format

`data/deposit-limits.json` has one object per state; `data/deposit-limits.csv` has the same columns.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `state`, `code` | State name and two-letter code (DC included) |
| `has_cap` | `true` for the 10 states with a deposit cap on some contracts |
| `deposit_limit` | The rule in one line |
| `cap_percent` | The cap as a percent of the contract price, when there is one (33.3 = one-third) |
| `cap_dollars` | A dollar cap, when the rule is "the lesser of" a percent and a dollar amount |
| `contracts_over` | The contract price above which the cap applies, when the law sets one |
| `cap_scope` | Exceptions and limits on the cap |
| `applies_to` | Who and what the law covers |
| `law`, `source_url` | The statute and where we read it |
| `confirmed` | `false` when the "no cap" finding was not confirmed against the statute |
| `notes` | What else the law says about deposits, escrow, refunds and payment schedules |
| `checked` | Date the statutes were checked |
